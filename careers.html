Why Vizit

A hard problem, a real market, and a team small enough that your work shows.

Every product on every digital shelf gets a fraction of a second to earn a click. For decades, the decision about which image to use came down to opinion. Vizit replaced the opinion with math — a model trained on how real shoppers respond, scoring the conversion power of content before a dollar is spent on it.

That means genuinely difficult work: computer vision at catalog scale, agentic systems that act on what they see, and a product used daily by the people who decide what the world's largest brands put in front of shoppers. Teams here are small and unusually senior, so the thing you build this quarter is in front of customers next quarter.

How we work

The habits we hire for.

01

Evidence over opinion

We built a company on replacing subjective review with data. We hold our own decisions to the same bar.

02

Ship, then sharpen

Real feedback comes from real usage. We get work in front of customers early and improve it in the open.

03

Own the outcome

Titles describe where you start, not where you stop. If it needs doing and you see it first, it is yours.

04

Customers in the room

Engineers join customer calls. Everyone hears the problem first-hand rather than through a summary.

05

Clarity is a kindness

Direct feedback, written decisions, no guessing games. Distributed teams run on things being said plainly.

06

Depth beats headcount

We stay deliberately small and hire people who raise the bar, rather than growing for the sake of it.

Our hiring process

Four conversations. No trick questions.

Most candidates go from first call to offer in two to three weeks. You will know who you are meeting and what each stage covers before it happens.

Step 01

Intro call

Thirty minutes with a recruiter on what you are looking for, what the role is, and whether the two line up.

Step 02

Hiring manager

A deeper conversation about the work itself with the person you would report to.

Step 03

Working session

A practical exercise close to the real job — reviewed together, never as unpaid homework.

Step 04

Team & decision

Meet future teammates, ask us anything, and get a clear yes or no with feedback either way.
